# Voice guide — how Adonai actually writes

Distilled from two real emails: one to his PI (Alex, English, warm/internal) and one
semi-cold approach to a former lecturer (Joan, Spanish). Reproduce **this** voice, not
a generic academic template.

## Core character
Warm but substantive. Every email carries real content — never just pleasantries.
Direct about what he wants, but never entitled. Concrete to the point of naming exact
tools, grades, dates and figures; he does not hand-wave. Confident about his ability
to learn fast, modest about his claim on anyone's time.

## Structural habits (follow this arc)
1. **Lead with the recipient's relevance**, not with himself. (Templeton's fit for the
   lab; Joan's specific courses and papers.) One or two sentences.
2. **Concrete self-introduction** — who he is, what he has actually done, with
   specifics (methods, tools, the lab, real numbers).
3. **The ask, stated plainly** — and scaled modestly ("a brief 15-20 minute meeting",
   "a coffee").
4. **Offer to reduce the other person's burden** — this is a signature move. ("I could
   make the application... you would only have to give the green light." / "my treat".)
5. **Pre-empt the no** — explicitly acknowledge it may not fit right now, without
   sounding defeated. ("Sé que quizá ahora mismo no tengas una plaza abierta con este
   perfil, y no lo doy por supuesto.")
6. **Warm close**, and occasionally a P.D./P.S. adding one extra concrete offer
   (attaching the thesis, offering the R code).

## Register: warm vs cold
- **Warm** (recipient IS in `network.yaml`): familiar, direct, first-name, can propose
  informal meetings and joint plans. Model on the Alex email.
- **Cold / semi-cold** (no tie in `network.yaml`): fuller self-introduction, explain how
  he came across their work, explicitly say he isn't assuming a position exists, ask for
  a short meeting. Model on the Joan email. **Default to this register whenever there is
  no warm tie on file.**

## Language
Match the recipient: **Spanish** for Spain-based recipients (and where he'd naturally use
it), **English** otherwise. State which language was chosen at the top of each draft so
he can override.

## Formatting habits
- Bulleted practical points when listing logistics (deadlines, amounts, eligibility).
- Includes real links when referencing an opportunity.
- Signs off with his full name; Spanish emails use "Un cordial saludo y gracias por tu
  tiempo,".
- Length: substantial but not padded — roughly 150-300 words for outreach.

## Hard rules (inherited from the scout)
- **Only claim a connection that exists in `network.yaml`.** Never infer a tie from a
  shared surname, institution or co-author. If there is no tie, write a cold email.
- **Only state CV facts written in `about_me.yaml`.** Everything else becomes a clearly
  marked `[BLANK: ...]` for him to fill — never a guess.
- Respect `confirm_with`: if an entry says someone must approve being named, surface
  that at the top of the draft as a checklist line, and do not name them in the body
  until confirmed.
- **Never send.** Drafts only.

## Anti-patterns (do NOT do these)
- Generic openers ("I hope this email finds you well", "I am writing to express my
  interest in...").
- Vague praise of the recipient's work without naming a specific paper or theme.
- Overclaiming ("expert in", "extensive experience") — he is an early-career RA and
  writes like one, leaning on demonstrable specifics instead.
- Padding the ask or burying it at the end.
